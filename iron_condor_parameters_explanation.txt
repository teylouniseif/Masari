IRON CONDOR STRATEGY PARAMETERS EXPLANATION
============================================

GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS
=====================
• IV (Implied Volatility): The market's expectation of how much a stock's price will move, 
  derived from option prices. Higher IV = market expects bigger price swings.

• Historical Volatility: How much a stock's price actually moved in the past, calculated 
  from historical price data.

• Daily Returns: The percentage change in stock price from one day to the next.
  FORMULA: (Today's Price - Yesterday's Price) / Yesterday's Price

• Bid-Ask Spread: The difference between what buyers will pay (bid) and sellers will 
  accept (ask). Tight spreads = good liquidity, wide spreads = poor liquidity.

• Open Interest: The total number of option contracts that are currently active and 
  haven't been exercised or expired yet.

• Theta Decay: The rate at which an option loses value as time passes. Options lose 
  value every day, which benefits option sellers.

• Gamma Risk: How quickly an option's delta (price sensitivity) changes as the stock 
  price moves. High gamma = rapid price changes in the option.

• Delta: How much an option's price changes when the stock price changes by $1.

• At-the-Money (ATM): An option where the strike price equals the current stock price.

• 30-Delta Options: Options with approximately 30% probability of being in-the-money 
  at expiration.

WHAT IS AN IRON CONDOR?
-----------------------
An iron condor is an options strategy that profits when a stock's price remains within a 
defined range until expiration. You simultaneously sell a put spread (betting the stock 
won't go too low) and a call spread (betting the stock won't go too high). The strategy 
collects premium upfront and profits if the stock stays between your defined strikes.

These parameters identify stocks with the highest probability of staying range-bound, 
maximizing the chance of profit while minimizing risk.

PRICE AND VOLUME CRITERIA
-------------------------
• min_price: $50.0 
  - COMPUTATION: Direct stock price from market data
  - RATIONALE: Below $50, option premiums become too small relative to transaction costs
  - RISK: Low-priced stocks often have wider spreads and less liquidity
  - EXAMPLE: A $30 stock might only offer $0.50 premium, but cost $1.00 in spreads

• max_price: $200.0 
  - COMPUTATION: Direct stock price from market data
  - RATIONALE: Above $200, margin requirements become excessive for retail traders
  - RISK: High-priced stocks require significant capital and have higher absolute risk
  - EXAMPLE: A $500 stock might require $50,000 in margin for a single contract

• min_volume: 1,000,000 
  - COMPUTATION: Average daily trading volume over the past 20 days
  - FORMULA: Sum(daily_volume) / 20
  - RATIONALE: High volume ensures tight bid-ask spreads and easy entry/exit
  - RISK: Low volume stocks have wide spreads and may not fill orders
  - EXAMPLE: AAPL trades ~50M shares daily vs. small caps trading 100K shares

• min_market_cap: $1B 
  - COMPUTATION: Market Cap = Current Stock Price × Total Shares Outstanding
  - RATIONALE: Large companies have more stable business models and less bankruptcy risk
  - RISK: Small caps can have extreme price movements and liquidity issues
  - EXAMPLE: Apple ($3T market cap) vs. penny stocks ($10M market cap)

VOLATILITY CRITERIA
-------------------
• min_implied_volatility: 20% 
  - COMPUTATION: Derived from option prices using the Black-Scholes model
  - FORMULA: IV = f(option_price, stock_price, strike, time_to_expiry, risk_free_rate)
  - RATIONALE: 20% IV provides sufficient premium to overcome transaction costs
  - INTERPRETATION: 20% IV means the market expects ±20% price movement annually
  - EXAMPLE: A stock at $100 with 20% IV expects to be between $80-$120 in one year

• max_implied_volatility: 50% 
  - COMPUTATION: Same as min_implied_volatility
  - RATIONALE: Above 50%, the stock is too unpredictable for reliable range prediction
  - RISK: High IV stocks can break out of ranges due to earnings, news, or market events
  - EXAMPLE: Biotech stocks often have 80%+ IV due to FDA approval uncertainty

• max_historical_volatility: 40% 
  - COMPUTATION: Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized
  - FORMULA: HV = std_dev(daily_returns) × √252
  - DAILY_RETURNS: (Price_today - Price_yesterday) / Price_yesterday
  - RATIONALE: Historical volatility predicts future price stability
  - INTERPRETATION: 40% HV means the stock moved ±40% in the past year
  - EXAMPLE: Utility stocks have ~15% HV, tech stocks have ~30-40% HV

• min_iv_skew: 5% 
  - COMPUTATION: Difference between call and put implied volatility at same strike
  - FORMULA: IV Skew = (IV_call - IV_put) / IV_put × 100
  - RATIONALE: Positive skew indicates market expects upside movement, providing opportunity
  - INTERPRETATION: 5% skew means calls are 5% more expensive than puts
  - EXAMPLE: Before earnings, calls often have higher IV than puts (positive skew)

LIQUIDITY CRITERIA
------------------
• min_option_volume: 1,000 
  - COMPUTATION: Total option contracts traded across all strikes and expirations
  - FORMULA: Sum(volume_for_all_options_of_stock)
  - RATIONALE: High volume ensures tight spreads and reliable fills
  - RISK: Low volume options may not fill or may fill at poor prices
  - EXAMPLE: SPY options trade 500K+ contracts daily vs. small stocks with 100 contracts

• min_open_interest: 500 
  - COMPUTATION: Total outstanding option contracts (not yet exercised or expired)
  - FORMULA: Sum(open_interest_for_all_options_of_stock)
  - RATIONALE: High open interest indicates market maker presence and tighter spreads
  - INTERPRETATION: 500 OI means 500 contracts are currently active
  - EXAMPLE: Liquid stocks have 10K+ OI, illiquid stocks have <100 OI

• max_bid_ask_spread: 10% 
  - COMPUTATION: (Ask Price - Bid Price) / Bid Price × 100
  - FORMULA: Spread% = (ask - bid) / bid × 100
  - RATIONALE: Wide spreads eat into profits and increase transaction costs
  - RISK: 10% spread means immediate 10% loss on round-trip trade
  - EXAMPLE: Liquid options have 1-2% spreads, illiquid options have 20%+ spreads

RANGE-BOUND CRITERIA
--------------------
• min_range_bound_score: 0.6 
  - COMPUTATION: Combines price range analysis and mean reversion metrics
  - FORMULA: Score = (range_score × 0.6) + (stability_score × 0.4)
  - RANGE_SCORE: 1 - (price_range / average_price) [0-1 scale]
  - STABILITY_SCORE: (1 + mean_reversion_correlation) / 2 [0-1 scale]
  - MEAN_REVERSION: Tendency of prices to return to average after moving away
  - RATIONALE: 0.6 indicates the stock has demonstrated range-bound behavior
  - INTERPRETATION: Higher scores = more predictable, lower scores = more volatile

• lookback_period: 90 days 
  - COMPUTATION: Number of trading days used for historical analysis
  - RATIONALE: 90 days captures recent market conditions without being too short
  - RISK: Too short (30 days) misses patterns, too long (1 year) includes outdated data
  - EXAMPLE: 90 days = ~4.5 months of trading data

TIME CRITERIA
-------------
• min_days_to_expiry: 30 
  - COMPUTATION: Calendar days from current date to option expiration
  - RATIONALE: 30+ days allows sufficient time for theta decay to work in your favor
  - RISK: Too short (7 days) = high gamma risk and low premium collection
  - EXAMPLE: 30 DTE options have ~70% of premium as time value

• max_days_to_expiry: 45 
  - COMPUTATION: Calendar days from current date to option expiration
  - RATIONALE: Beyond 45 days, gamma risk increases and time value becomes less predictable
  - RISK: Longer time = more exposure to unexpected events (earnings, news, market crashes)
  - EXAMPLE: 45 DTE options have ~60% of premium as time value

WHY THESE MATTER FOR IRON CONDORS
=================================
1. RANGE-BOUND STOCKS: Stocks with low historical volatility and mean-reverting behavior 
   are less likely to break out of defined ranges, reducing assignment risk

2. HIGH LIQUIDITY: Tight bid-ask spreads and high volume ensure efficient entry/exit 
   without significant slippage, preserving profit margins

3. MODERATE VOLATILITY: Sufficient IV provides attractive premiums while keeping 
   breakout risk manageable and predictable

4. PROPER TIME FRAME: 30-45 DTE optimizes theta decay (time value erosion) while 
   minimizing gamma risk (sensitivity to price changes)

5. REASONABLE PRICE RANGE: Manageable position sizes and margin requirements allow 
   for proper risk management and portfolio diversification

TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
==============================
• All metrics are calculated using real-time market data from Questrade API
• Historical volatility uses 90-day lookback with daily returns
• IV skew calculation uses 30-delta options for consistency
• Range-bound score combines multiple statistical measures for robustness
• All thresholds are empirically derived from backtesting results

The goal is to identify stocks that exhibit predictable, range-bound behavior with 
sufficient liquidity and volatility to generate consistent premium income while 
minimizing the risk of catastrophic losses from unexpected price movements.
